The problem

Today a market exists because an exchange lists it — and dies when the exchange delists it.

Two parties who both hold inventory still cannot settle against each other without handing custody to a third.

Bitcoin settles value. It deliberately refuses to express the conditions of a trade.

Today

asset → listing committee → market

Born by permission. Dies by decision.

On BATHRON

inventory + quotes → market

Born when two parties can settle. Nobody can revoke what nobody granted.

The target open network

Several operators. Several providers. No privileged one.

BATHRON is being built toward a public network with several independent Consensus Operators, several independent Settlement, Clearing and Liquidity Providers, and competing applications — where no operator chooses what settles and a provider can disappear without taking the instrument with it.

That network is not deployed. Application building is open today; Consensus-Operator admission is not, and the exact admission mechanism is deliberately left open rather than invented. The target open network →

What contracts can verify

Facts Bitcoin can prove — checked by every node.

A script can assert five classes of Bitcoin fact, verified against headers the protocol carries in consensus. No designated oracle takes part.

  • Difficulty at a Bitcoin height, above or below a threshold.
  • Height reached — a buried Bitcoin height.
  • Median-time-past — Bitcoin’s own clock.
  • A confirmed payment — a Bitcoin transaction paying a given script, Merkle-proved and buried deep enough.

The limits matter as much. These are predicates, not readings: a script asserts difficulty ≥ X, it cannot read the number. Binary and barrier payoffs are native; a linear one must be decomposed. Only history buried 144 Bitcoin blocks is readable. The five predicates and their limits →

Programmable settlement

Constrain what a spend may become.

Covenants, including a recursive pair; constraints on output amounts and scripts; hashlocks and timelocks; and signature verification over arbitrary messages. Active in consensus and tested — which is not the same as having a product that uses them.

External attestations. For anything Bitcoin cannot prove — a price, an outside event — the engine can check a signed attestation, and supports discreet-log-contract shapes with no new opcode. BATHRON verifies the signature; it can never make the statement true.

Confidentiality, and its limit. Shielded transfers move value without publishing it. The confidentiality of covenant-bearing settlement is not demonstrated. Covenants, DLC and confidentiality →

What can be built

Markets are one card in the deck.

  • Markets with no listing committee — a pair appears because someone quotes it.
  • Mining-difficulty hedges — settled on a predicate every node checks.
  • Escrows opened by a buried Bitcoin payment.
  • Time-bound agreements keyed to Bitcoin’s clock.
  • Anything indexed on a price — not native: no price oracle, no margin engine, no liquidation in consensus.

How a market appears

No listing committee stands between you and a market.

1

Bring inventory

The asset to pair, and M1.

2

Publish quotes

Signed, off-chain, on any relay. The consensus never sees them.

3

Settle

A counterparty accepts. The M1 leg settles on BATHRON; an external-asset leg settles on its native chain under linked conditions.

4

Others join

Another provider quotes the same pair, tighter or deeper. No invitation needed.

BTC/M1·PIVX/M1·DOGE/M1·LTC/M1·external asset / M1 — integration required

Native BTC ⇄ M1 is paired with hashlocked legs on both chains sharing one preimage — the components are demonstrated on testnet; a generally atomic client flow is not yet claimed. A chain that supports hashlocks and timelocks can be paired the same way — but each chain needs its own application work: script shapes, timelock ordering, reorganisation handling and a provider holding both sides. Only the Bitcoin pair has been demonstrated.

Why Bitcoin stays at the center

In one cross-chain pattern, Bitcoin sees two transactions and BATHRON settles what happens between them.

Bitcoin is the final asset — the reserve of value and the only origin of M1: BTC → M0 → M1, one way, verified by SPV inside consensus.

BATHRON never replaces Bitcoin and never holds it. There is no reserve and no redemption — what brings native BTC back is a market, not a vault.

Bitcoin facts are checked by every node. No designated oracle is needed for native Bitcoin facts — difficulty, height, median-time-past, confirmed payment. External prices and real-world events still require external attestations.

Who is who

Operators

Run consensus: produce blocks, sign finality, publish facts about themselves. One operator, one vote. They never rank, never choose a provider, never set a price.

Settlement Providers

Participants, not administrators. A Clearing Provider quotes and orchestrates; a Liquidity Provider holds inventory and prices a pair. They appear and disappear freely.

You

Build an application, propose a settlement flow, or simply settle. Reading and verifying the chain asks nothing of you but a node; producing blocks requires collateral and an operator registration that is not yet open.

Where we are

Experimental. Public testnet live. No mainnet.

Anyone can build, quote, pair and settle without asking permission. Operator admission is not yet open — the current operator set is project-run while the open-admission threat model is worked. No external audit yet. No proven market.
