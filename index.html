Facts, not opinions

Bitcoin already proves things. Now a contract can use them.

One settlement pivot

Users keep their assets. Providers use M1.

Pairing each of N assets with M1 needs N connections instead of a full mesh of direct pairs. One M1 inventory connects a provider to all quoted pairs and LPs, subject to their available liquidity and terms.

Why burn BTC?

Verified BTC destruction creates M0, with no premine. Locking M0 one for one creates M1: working capital for settlement. Providers can also buy existing M0 or M1 from holders.

Unlocking M1 returns M0, not BTC. The burn is irreversible, creates no redeemable BTC reserve and guarantees no market peg or price floor.

Open markets

A pair exists because someone quotes it.

Providers publish offers from their inventory. A second provider can quote the same pair without an invitation or listing permission. Announcements, relays and indexers help discovery outside consensus; published service facts inform reputation, without a protocol ranking.

Example: stablecoin → BTC

Alice sends stablecoin X and receives BTC. The LP quotes X/M1 and supplies M1 to the SP, which quotes BTC/M1 and supplies BTC. Alice keeps her keys and never needs to hold M1.

This route requires compatible hashlocks and timed refunds on X's chain, funded quotes, ordered deadlines and active monitoring. Each external leg follows its own chain's rules; recovery requires a transaction.

Distinct roles, shared rules

Know whom you rely on, and for what.

Operators

A registered identity requires a burned ticket and maturity. This visible, irreversible entry cost is separate from settlement inventory. Operators can produce blocks and participate in finality.

Settlement Providers · BTC/M1

The SP quotes and orchestrates BTC/M1, including the Bitcoin leg. An Operator identity is required; block production is optional.

Liquidity Providers · X/M1

The LP quotes X/M1, supplies liquidity and manages the external asset leg. An Operator identity is optional.

Builders define applications, users choose terms, and observers verify independently. An Operator can also provide a service; the identity-to-service link is public and the same transaction rules apply.

Objectively proven double signing permanently bans the Operator identity, with no slashing. The burned ticket remains spent.
